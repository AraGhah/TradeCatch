# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The sentence exists. "You're on the job. We'll handle the calls." passes the one-sentence test, and the three failure moments on the site (the unanswered call, the lost details, the quiet quote) are written as experiences, not features.
- It was built from the caller's moment backward. Answering under the contractor's name, in the caller's language, is the right place to start: the homeowner never meets TradeCatch, only the plumber's business picking up on the second ring.
- Real "no"s. No call transfer, no voice cloning, no bulk messaging, no CRM, no diagnosing safety conditions. Two plans with one difference between them (booking). That is discipline most one-person products lack.
- One pair of hands owns every touchpoint: the audit, every message the contractor approves before it goes live, scenario testing, monitoring, billing, help centre. Confirm-before-billing on overages is craft in a place nobody looks.

## What would kill it
1. **The call itself is unmeasured.** The brief describes a browser test of the dashboard and says nothing about the product the homeowner actually hears: Québec French with a street address and an apartment number, a caller who switches languages mid-sentence, a cellphone in a basement. Every stumble is charged to the contractor's name, and with no call transfer there is no escape hatch. The caller hangs up and dials the next company, which is the exact failure the product sells against.
2. **The promise breaks at the seam where it matters most.** The headline says 24/7; the fine print says emergency handling stays with the contractor. So at 11pm on a Saturday the receptionist flags urgent, texts a contractor who is asleep, and a homeowner with water through the ceiling has just been told by "the plumber" that someone will be in touch. The hand-off from flag to alert to human is undescribed. Professional carries a cousin of the same seam: booking into a Google Calendar that many contractors do not maintain.
3. **Corners cut where the customer can see.** Missing translations in a product whose thesis is bilingual Québec. Validation gaps in a product whose job is capturing addresses and numbers correctly. About to ship behind a $1,999.99 setup fee, which promises polish. And the founder is the first five minutes, the setup, and the monitoring: while the founder is on a demo call, nobody is watching the live receptionist.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Play three real calls (with consent): Québec French with an address, English, one language switch. Of all live calls so far, what share captured name, number, address, service and urgency with no correction from the contractor?
- Walk me minute by minute through the 11pm burst pipe. What does the homeowner hear, what does the contractor's phone do, and what happens if nobody acknowledges within fifteen minutes?
- Was call transfer cut for focus, or because it was hard?
- On Professional, did the last contractor you audited actually keep a calendar? What does the receptionist do when it is empty all week?
- Which open bug would embarrass you most if the first paying customer found it? Why is it still open at launch?
- What does the $699.99 website service add to the one thing? Would you cut it?
- Is missed-call text-back a fallback for when the voice receptionist fails, or a separate mode? How often does it fire?
- At the minute cap, what does the receptionist do: stop answering? The cap figures and per-minute cost belong to `/founder-cfo`; the experience at the cap belongs to you.

Open questions for other skills: what Montréal contractors use today and how a homeowner reacts to an AI voice under a trade name (`/founder-consumer`); whether competitors offer a human fallback or transfer, and at what price (`/founder-competitors`).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. No paying customer goes live until every high-severity bug is closed and no screen a contractor or caller touches has a missing translation.
2. A written, night-tested urgent-call path with a defined behaviour when the contractor does not acknowledge, tested from a real phone, not a browser.
3. A measured capture-accuracy rate from real recorded calls in Québec French, with a target the founder sets and meets before selling past the first hand-held cohort.
4. The website service out of the launch offer.
5. Professional setup includes a calendar-hygiene step and a defined empty-calendar behaviour.
