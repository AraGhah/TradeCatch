# Offers lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pain is priced in the buyer's own units: a rung-out call is a job gone to the next name on Google; a $9,000 quote nobody chased. A plumber pictures that without a slide.
- It does not compete on price. Québec French in the caller's language, every message approved by the contractor, a named person doing the install: a US-first AI receptionist cannot copy that on a pricing page.
- The stack is built, not promised: 24/7 bilingual answering, request capture with urgency flag, missed-call text-back, follow-up texts, calendar booking, dashboard, referral program, and a live demo a buyer can call.
- Month-to-month billing with overage only on confirmation keeps recurring sacrifice low.
- Real scarcity exists (one person does every install); the offer does not use it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **$1,999.99 before any proof, with no guarantee.** The value equation lives or dies on perceived likelihood. The brief gives no customer count, no captured-call numbers, no testimonial, yet asks the largest cheque first. A one-truck plumber is asked to pay $2,000 for a voice he has never heard talk to his customers in French. The live demo is not a result he can check.
2. **Time to result and buyer effort run through one calendar.** Audit, configure, write every message with the customer, test every scenario with the crew, then monitor. The brief does not say how many days pass from checkout to the first live answered call, or how many hours the contractor gives up. The lens punishes delay and effort; this delivery model concentrates both and caps installs per month at one person's output.
3. **"Answers every call" is unpriced at the margin.** Allowances and per-minute voice cost are unknown. If a busy HVAC line in January hits the cap and overage needs the contractor's confirmation, the 11 p.m. no-heat call is either unanswered (the one outcome the buyer paid to avoid) or served unbilled. Either breaks the offer. `/founder-cfo` owns this.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many paying customers today, and for the first one: calls captured, flagged urgent, appointments booked in 30 days?
- Days from checkout to first live answered call? Founder hours per install?
- When the allowance is spent and no overage is confirmed, what does the receptionist do on the next call?
- Why $1,999.99 up front, rather than credited back over months three to twelve or refunded if a stated result is missed?
- Which trade said yes fastest in demos? Landscaping does not lose jobs in twenty seconds the way plumbing and HVAC do. Why not lead with one?
- No call transfer: when a caller says "I need someone now," what is said?
- Does "text follow-up" chase the $9,000 quote the site describes, or only confirm the captured request? If it chases quotes, name it.
- What does a bilingual human answering service in Montréal charge per month? Open; `/founder-competitors`. Without it, "double the price and still the obvious choice" is untestable.
- How many contractors in the four cities are reachable, given the pitch targets people who do not answer the phone? Open; `/founder-consumer`.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A printed guarantee on the setup fee: refunded or credited against monthly fees if a stated result (requests captured on Essential, appointments booked on Professional) is not reached within 60 days. The founder sets the number; it goes on the pricing page.
2. Three paying customers in one lead trade (plumbing or HVAC) with before/after call counts quotable in a demo.
3. Allowance per plan and cost per answered minute confirmed by `/founder-cfo`, with a cap rule that never leaves a call unanswered.
4. One trade leads the message for the first twenty customers; the other four stay on the site, not in the headline.
5. Real onboarding capacity per month stated on the site; it is the only urgency this offer can honestly claim.
6. `/founder-competitors` confirms $249 to $349 is a premium position, not an accidental price-leader against human answering services.
