# Monopoly lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The market is drawn small enough to own: owner-operated trades in four named municipalities, served in Québec French and English. The Monopoly lens prefers a big share of a small market to a sliver of a large one, and this qualifies.
- The product is built and in production: billing, confirm-before-overage usage caps, calendar booking, referral program, help centre. This is not a deck.
- The high-touch install (audit the call and quote flow, write every message with the owner, test every scenario, monitor after launch) is know-how generic self-serve tools do not ship. It also builds switching cost: a leaving contractor gives up configured scripts, approved wording, a booking flow and a forwarded number.
- The $1,999.99 setup fee pays for that install rather than eating it, and pricing above generic tools fits a non-commodity position if the difference holds.
- Of the four durable advantages (technology, network effects, scale, brand), a founder-named local brand is the one this business can plausibly build. Trades hire by word of mouth; a name that travels between Laval plumbers is one a national vendor cannot buy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The core is rented, so the ten-times edge is a service, not technology.** Twilio, Stripe, Cloudflare and a third-party voice model are open to anyone. Generic AI answering tools already exist, and the brief does not establish that they handle Québec French badly. What stops a Montréal agency in month six bundling a generic tool, French prompts and a setup afternoon for less? Today, only the founder's name, with no stated customers behind it.
2. **Distribution and delivery share one calendar.** Demo, install, monitoring and bug fixes are all founder hours. The first 100 customers need 100 demos and 100 high-touch installs from one person. "Big share of a small market" is unreachable at that throughput, and the install cannot be cut because it is the differentiator. Hours per install are unknown.
3. **The beachhead is not picked.** Five trades across four municipalities in two languages is a region, not a wedge. The referral program becomes a channel only when customers are dense enough to know each other. How many contractors fit and are reachable is open (`/founder-consumer`); who else sells to the same buyer, at what price, is open (`/founder-competitors`).

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Who else sells this to a Laval plumber today, at what monthly price, in what French? Have you tested the two nearest generic tools against yours with real Québec callers? (`/founder-competitors`)
- What is the secret: what did contractors tell you that the generic tools got wrong?
- How many owner-operated trade businesses fit the profile in the four municipalities, and how many can you reach by demo in a year? (`/founder-consumer`)
- Hours per install, hours per customer per month, cost per demo. At what customer count does your calendar run out? (`/founder-cfo`)
- Per-minute voice and telephony cost against the allowance: gross margin on Essential at $249 and Professional at $349. (`/founder-cfo`)
- The site names "the quote that goes quiet" as lost money. Is quote chasing in either plan, or only on the page?
- Which trade and town come first, and what share there means you own it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. One beachhead named: one trade, one or two municipalities, a share target set after `/founder-consumer` sizes it. No new region until that target is met.
2. A competitor map with prices from `/founder-competitors`, plus a recorded head-to-head Québec French test against the two nearest generic tools with real contractor callers.
3. Unit economics from `/founder-cfo`: per-minute cost, allowance per plan, gross margin per plan, hours per install, hours per customer per month, and the resulting one-person customer ceiling.
4. A productized install: per-trade script and scenario templates so the second plumber takes a fraction of the first's hours, without dropping the founder-tested promise.
5. One paying customer sourced by referral rather than founder outreach before any marketing spend. The brand is the moat; show it moves on its own.
