## The story

A plumber cannot interrupt a repair to answer his ringing phone. TradeCatch answers, qualifies a new customer's plumbing problem, books an appointment, and sends a confirmation. Only after completing his repair does the plumber pick up his phone and discover the booking.

The contractor never answers a call, taps a call-control button, speaks to the caller, or approves the booking. The receptionist is an off-screen female voice throughout.

Customer continuity: one adult homeowner, about 35–45, casual everyday clothing, in a distinctly different residential kitchen. The customer is Alex Roy; their service address is 24 Cedar Lane. ## Audio direction

- Narrator: confident adult male, warm and matter-of-fact, neutral Canadian English. Avoid a hard sales pitch.
- Receptionist: a single adult female voice, warm Canadian French pronunciation and fluent North American English. Language changes, identity and microphone character stay consistent. Never use an on-screen face for her.
- Customer: everyday delivery, subtle concern becoming relief. Natural inhalations and response gaps; no cartoon gestures or performative excitement.
- Telephone perspective: receptionist is heard through the customer's handset with gentle telephone coloration while remaining intelligible. Narrator stays full-band and centered. Never play a phone-processed version and clean version of the same line simultaneously.
- Foley: believable ringtone and vibration, restrained wrench/pipe handling, occasional faucet drops, one DTMF tone, handwashing, one soft booking notification. No ringing after the receptionist begins.
- Music: subtle licensed instrumental bed with a gentle lift at the booking reveal. Duck under every spoken line. No artificial drone substituted for finished commercial music.
- Target web mix: approximately −16 LUFS integrated, true peak at or below −1 dBTP; verify on phone speakers and headphones. Preserve room tone between dialogue turns.
