# TradeCatch — Shot prompts

These are generation instructions, not evidence that footage has been produced. Edit lengths below are targets; request enough extra motion at each end for clean cuts. Generate individual shots, retaining one approved character reference per actor and fixed environment references. The final video is a 75-second assembly, not one 75-second generative take.

## Shared visual direction

Photorealistic live-action Canadian contractor commercial. Real human appearance, believable body mechanics and facial microexpressions, visible natural skin texture, ordinary residential settings, professional documentary cinematography, soft available daylight, restrained neutral-warm grading, realistic depth of field. 16:9 composition with people and key actions protected for a later vertical reframe. No cartoons, animation, CGI-looking humans, avatars, robots, holograms, artificial skin, exaggerated expressions, floaty movement, impossible tools, distorted hands, brand watermarks, captions, or generated readable screen text. Add exact UI and brand copy during editing.

Contractor reference: same approximately 38-year-old male plumber, short dark hair, light stubble, navy work shirt, charcoal work trousers, brown work boots. Customer reference: same adult homeowner in casual clothing in a second, clearly different kitchen. Keep faces, wardrobe, hands, props, architecture, lighting, and phone models consistent within each location.

## A — Work continues, 00:00–00:12

**A1 · Establishing medium shot · 4 seconds used.** In a believable Canadian kitchen with cream cabinetry and pale counters, the plumber kneels at an open sink cabinet. Both hands are occupied with a small wrench and a visible pipe fitting; a shallow catch tray sits underneath. He makes one small controlled adjustment. Gentle handheld camera, natural window light. Smartphone rests untouched on the counter above him. The actor is focused and unposed.

**A2 · Phone insert · 3 seconds used.** A smartphone lies screen-up on the same kitchen counter. A subtle vibration produces tiny realistic movement. The screen is a neutral dark plate for a later incoming-call composite. Shallow depth of field, partial soft view of the working plumber beyond. No hand enters frame. He does not answer.

**A3 · Contractor reaction · 5 seconds used.** Same man and kitchen, medium-low angle. Both hands still hold the tool and fitting. He briefly glances toward the off-screen ringing phone, then calmly returns his eyes to the repair. His hands never leave the work, reach upward, or touch any phone or ear. Small ordinary movement, no frustration pantomime. Ringing stops in post, without any gesture from him.

## B — A caller is helped, 00:12–00:27

**B1 · Caller introduction · about 4 seconds used.** Same homeowner reference in a distinctly different lived-in kitchen, seated near a slowly dripping chrome faucet. They hold a smartphone to their ear and listen. Slight concern, natural breathing and eye movement, no speech during the receptionist greeting. Daylight, medium close-up, quiet domestic surroundings.

**B2 · Keypad insert · about 1–2 seconds used.** Customer lowers the phone naturally and presses the digit 1 once with a thumb, then returns it to their ear. Use a neutral screen plate and add the keypad in post. Natural hand anatomy, no extra taps, no contact with hang-up. This must be the customer's hand, never the contractor's.

**B3 · Customer performance · dialogue coverage.** Same customer and location, natural conversational close-up. Spoken line: “Hi, my kitchen faucet keeps dripping.” Pause, listening to the receptionist. Later response: “All day.” Modest concern, tiny glance toward the faucet, natural human lip synchronization. Generate separate performances if the production tool cannot support timed turns. Keep the receptionist entirely off-screen.

**B4 · Faucet cutaway · about 2 seconds used.** Close shot of a normal chrome kitchen faucet and basin in the customer's kitchen. A single drop forms and falls, followed by another after a believable pause. No flood, no impossible water behavior. This can cover the receptionist asking whether the leak is constant.

## C — Booked automatically, 00:27–00:45

**C1 · Customer listening and response · about 7 seconds used.** Same homeowner in the same position, listening to an appointment offer. Natural small nod. Spoken response: “Perfect.” Then a listening pause. No speaking during the receptionist's question.

**C2 · Details collected · about 4 seconds used.** Same customer close-up. They clearly and casually say, “Alex Roy. Twenty-four Cedar Lane.” Relaxed eye line, normal pause after name. This is fictitious demonstration information.

**C3 · Product insert · about 7 seconds used.** Use actual TradeCatch demonstration interface capture or an accurate composited interface rather than generated footage of text. Show captured customer details, service request, a checked availability entry, tomorrow at 2:30 PM booked, then SMS sent. Keep it one coherent product view with restrained state changes. Clean navy, warm paper, and orange branding. An earlier availability check may appear as an activity-history item. No contractor interaction and no floating holograms.

## D — The work is finished, 00:45–01:00

**D1 · Repair complete · about 2 seconds used.** Same plumber, same tools and kitchen. He stops his small wrench adjustment, puts the tool in the tray, and begins to stand. Ordinary effort and balance. Phone stays on the counter, untouched.

**D2 · Cleanup · about 3 seconds used.** Natural insert of the plumber rinsing his hands at the sink, then drying them with a towel. Maintain wardrobe and daylight. Cut between actions to indicate elapsed time. No tools in his hands now.

**D3 · Notification · about 4 seconds used.** After drying his hands, the plumber picks up the counter phone for the first time. Over-the-shoulder close-up of a neutral phone-screen plate. Composite TradeCatch notification: “New Appointment Booked” / “Kitchen Faucet Repair” / “Tomorrow — 2:30 PM”. He taps that notification once. Display booking details afterward, never a call screen.

**D4 · Quiet payoff · about 6 seconds used, may overlap D3.** Same man looks down at the booked appointment and gives a small, believable satisfied smile. He does not talk into the phone or put it to his ear. Natural pause, then lowers the phone and gathers his things. No celebration or exaggerated sales-ad expression.

## E — Closing, 01:00–01:15

**E1 · Exterior · about 4 seconds used; may begin at 00:58.5.** Same plumber walks naturally from the customer's modest Canadian home toward his unbranded work truck. Work bag in one hand, phone pocketed. Soft afternoon light, cinematic stabilized wide shot, realistic gait and grounded shadows. Frame the actor and truck access near center for a later vertical version. No on-camera speaking.

**E2 · End card · 11 seconds.** Use the supplied clean end-card artwork with the existing TradeCatch mark. Optional brief opacity transition; otherwise keep it steady and readable.
